# The path, and why it is in this order

## The problem this is shaped around

An adult who has tried to learn for several years and not got anywhere has usually not failed at playing. They have failed at one of these: practising without knowing whether it was any good, practising something too hard for too long, or stopping.

So the path is built to make each of those harder. Every take is measured, so you always know. The tempo ladder decides when to speed up, so you are never grinding at a tempo you cannot play. And material from the two songs appears as early as it can be played, so progress is always visibly going somewhere rather than toward a distant payoff.

## Two ordering principles

**One new demand at a time.** The plucking hand before the fretting hand. Pitch before rhythm. Rhythm before both at once. Each unit adds exactly one thing that was not there before.

**Song material as early as it is playable.** Angel's riff is two notes, so it unlocks immediately after the rhythm lesson that makes it possible — long before the melodies, which are musically simpler but technically harder, and which wait on the shapes lesson. It is listed under The songs, but its only prerequisite is rhythm. The hymn's roots arrive as soon as the shapes lesson has covered the positions they use.

## The units

### Foundations

**Get set up.** Before anything else, the instrument is checked. Three of the most common reasons a beginner decides they have no aptitude are a buzzing fret, an action too high to press, and a bass that is out of tune — all three feel exactly like failing, and none of them is. Then the input, then latency calibration, which is what makes every later timing judgment honest.

**The plucking hand.** Finding each string by feel, making a consistent sound, and damping the strings that are not being played. Alternating index and middle from the first session, because using one finger for everything feels easier now and caps your speed permanently. Muting is taught here rather than later because two strings ringing at once have no single pitch: the app cannot name the note, and the feedback it gives instead is about the wrong thing.

**Reading tab.** Tablature, taught on the four open strings and nothing else. It arrives here rather than later because it is the notation both target songs are written in, and because the app puts a tab chart under every exercise from the first one onward — a learner who cannot read it is being shown a notation nobody has explained. Teaching it on open strings means the only new thing is the reading; the hands already know how to make these four sounds.

**The fretting hand.** Pressing just behind the fret, one finger per fret, and a thumb position that keeps the little finger in reach. The little finger is weak, which is why it needs using rather than avoiding.

### Fundamentals

**Finding notes.** The E and A strings, which is where both songs live. The dots at 3, 5, 7, 9 and 12 are the only landmarks, and fret 5 of any string matching the next string open is a free way to check yourself.

**Playing in time.** Quarter notes, eighth notes, rests, and the sixteenth-note split that Angel is built on. Counting out loud, because counting silently is a different and much harder skill that hides mistakes from you.

**Shapes that move.** Octaves, root and fifth, the major and minor scales. The bass is tuned in equal fourths, so a shape keeps its sound anywhere you move it: learn the shape, and the note names follow.

### Playing music

**Three melodies you already know** — Ode to Joy, Twinkle, Happy Birthday. Playing something whose sound you know is how you learn to hear your own mistakes without being told. Each stays in one hand position, so the work is the melody and the timing rather than moving around the neck.

### The songs

**Angel**, in four stages: eight even eighths, then the lift to D, then the sixteenth split, then the same thing on the E string for the tone. Two notes and six minutes.

**This Is the Christ, roots**, then **the full line**. Roots first is not a lesser version — it is what a bass player would actually play behind congregational singing. The full line is the published piano left hand, moved into the bass guitar's register.

## How a session should go

The default daily goal is fifteen minutes. That is deliberate: in the clearest study on the question — postal workers learning to type, total hours held constant — one hour once a day beat two hours twice a day by a wide margin, and the most effective schedule was the one trainees rated least satisfying. Short and daily wins, and it does not feel like it is winning.

A short day is a real day: the streak counts any completed take, however brief. That is what keeps the habit alive on a bad week.

The app orders each day as reviews first by urgency, then whatever is already underway, then new material to fill out a short plan. Reviews come first because a skill sliding backward costs more than a new one gained, and because you should not have to decide this each day.

## What progress means here

The number on the dashboard is the **retention score**: takes played with the scrolling highway, the fretboard marker and the live note readout all switched off.

This matters more than it sounds. Accuracy measured while all of that is helping is inflated by exactly that help, which means a product that reports in-session accuracy as progress is optimising against its own outcome. A review that is due defaults to a retention check for this reason.

The second number is how often your guess about your own timing matched the measurement. After each take you are asked whether you were ahead, behind or on it, before you are shown the answer. That two-second gap is the best-evidenced single intervention in the motor-learning literature, and it is undone by filling the gap with anything — which is why nothing else is on the screen at that moment.

## The exercises

Spread across ten lessons, in `drills.ts`, `melodies.ts`, and generated from the song charts by `songExercises.ts` — so correcting a transcription corrects every drill built on it in the same edit.

Every exercise declares the skills it trains, and the review scheduler works on those tags, so nothing drops out of rotation once it has been learned.
