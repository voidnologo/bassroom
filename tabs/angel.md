# Angel — bass

**Massive Attack feat. Horace Andy** · *Mezzanine*, 1998
Transcribed for personal study.

**Tempo:** ♩ = 107 (the drums are in half time, so it *feels* like 54) · **Key:** C minor · **Meter:** 4/4
**Tuning:** standard (E A D G)

## Why this is a good first song

The entire six-minute bass part is **two notes**: C, and the D a whole tone above it. There is almost no fingering to learn. Everything that makes the line work is rhythm — which means all of your attention goes to the thing that is actually hard, and none of it goes to finding notes.

It also never stops. There is not one rest bar in the song. That makes it an honest stamina test for the plucking hand, and a good reason to practise it in two-minute blocks rather than trying to play it end to end on day one.

## The riff

One bar, repeated. Counted in sixteenths — "**1** e **&** a **2** e **&** **a** **3** e **&** a **4** e **&** a" — the attacks fall on 1, &, 2, & , a, 3, &, 4, and the last & is the D.

In words: steady eighth notes on C, except that the "and" of beat 2 splits into two sixteenths, and the last eighth of the bar steps up to D before falling back to C on the next downbeat.

That single extra sixteenth is what gives the riff its lope. Every simplified tab flattens it to eight straight eighths, which is playable and sounds wrong.

## Fingering

**A string, frets 3 and 5.** Index finger on 3, ring finger on 5. Nothing moves. This is the easy version and it is the one to learn the rhythm on.

**E string, frets 8 and 10.** Identical pitches. The thicker string is darker and fatter, which is what the record sounds like. Switch once the rhythm is automatic.

## Playing notes

- **Count eighths out loud** against a 107 bpm metronome, or set it to 54 and feel each click as half a bar. Do not try to feel it at 107 as quarter notes — the track does not work that way, and neither will your counting.
- **Even and slightly detached.** No note is louder than the others. The groove is in the subdivision and in the lift to D.
- **Fingerstyle**, right hand near the neck. A pick is too bright for this unless you filter a lot of top off afterwards.
- **The octave drops are out of reach.** The record moves the riff down an octave for long stretches, to a C at 33 Hz — below the open E string on a 4-string bass. Play the whole song in the octave written here. Nothing is lost that a 4-string could have played anyway.
- **Left-hand muting on the downbeat** is the dub gesture in the lower-octave sections: beat 1 is felt rather than heard, and the pulse re-articulates after it. Worth knowing about; not worth attempting until the straight version is solid.
