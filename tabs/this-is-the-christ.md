## The shape of the song

| Bars | Section | Meter | Key |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pickup — "They heard a". Bass is silent. | 3/4 | E♭ |
| 2–11 | Verse | 3/4 | E♭ major |
| 12 | Turn | 4/4 | E♭ → C |
| 13–25 | Chorus — "This is the Christ" | 4/4 | C → F major |

The verse is in E♭ and the chorus ends in F, a whole tone higher. That lift, arriving exactly on the words the hymn is named for, is the point of the piece. Your part makes the move with it: the verse ends on a B♭ and the chorus begins on C.

## The bars worth isolating

**Bars 4 and 5.** A♭ down to G down to F, then G back up through A to B. The only stepwise motion in the verse, and the thing that makes the line sound like a bass part rather than a list of roots. Learn these two bars before anything else in Level 2.

**Bar 15, beat 3.** An F♯ — the only sharp in the whole hymn. It is the third of D7 and it leans into the G in bar 16. E string, second fret.

**Bar 19.** B♭ minor, but the bass sits on F, the fifth, for three beats before dropping to D♭. Resist the urge to "correct" it to a B♭; the F is what the hymn asks for.

**Bars 13–25 generally.** The entire chorus lives inside the first six frets. If your hand is travelling, you are playing it somewhere it does not need to be played.

## How to practise this

1. Roots, verse only, no metronome. Find each note. Let it ring.
2. Roots, verse only, metronome at 50. Change on beat 1 of each bar.
3. Add the chorus roots. Notice your hand barely moves.
4. Bring it to 63, the slow end of the marked tempo.
5. Swap bars 4 and 5 for the Level 2 line. Leave everything else as roots.
6. Add Level 2 bar by bar, hardest first: 19, then 16, then 14, then the rest.

At ♩ = 63 the hymn runs a little over ninety seconds. Once you can get through it without stopping, play it against the recording in the Church's music library.
